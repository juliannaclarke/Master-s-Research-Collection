# Interview responses
## 1
I know exactly which door to get off at to be right in front of the staircase at my stop, its usually the second car, first car, and I would say that anyone that has been commuting in NYC for more than like a year probably knows where to go on the platform before getting on the train to be at their staircase at their destination

## 2
I always try and sit in the seats across from the back door, I like being close to the exit so I can dismount quickly and be able to see my surroundings in the bus

I always bring my lanyard with me which holds my bus pass so I don't lose it. I never carry my bus pass loose

I try and say hello or good morning/evening or give the driver a nod!

If a bus is early I usually take it as a bad sign, as it will likely throw off the flow and alignment of my commute because the transfers won't line up as I like them to

I wouldn't say I'm a particularly superstitious person myself but I could see how my habits can be seen that way 🙂

## 3
hmm, I'm not sure I'm all that superstitious about it given how much I know. no particular item for transit besides all the stuff I always take with me. favourite seats only barely for VIA Rail - I usually select forward facing with the best window alignment, so usually 10-15 A/D depending on the equipment, car, and whether it'll be daytime when I pass TMC. 
I'm not sure if there's anything specific superstition-wise that'll mess up my trip but if I leave the house at like 3:30pm it's a bad time

## 4

seeing the bus lay over at the stop here in toronto (not something ive seen in standard practice in ottawa) gives me more assurance when im checking the live data to see if i should hurry

## 5

do you have one specific seat that you always choose? where and why?

Normally just door-based, as in, if I know where I'm getting off, and I know where the exits at that station are, I get on and sit near the door closest to the exits. But, if the train is particularly empty or only at the ends, there are two seats facing a window into the drivers' cab that I like, because I can watch out the front of the train.

do you always bring something with you on public transit?

Myself and my bag, basically.

is there something you do when you get on board a train/bus?

Pay my fare if I haven't already. Then I look if there are any set of unoccupied seats and I sit there if I can. Getting off, I make sure to thank the bus driver if I am near the front.

if something specific happens, does it assure you that you will have a good trip/bad trip?

Not really, aside from something big like a breakdown/emergency.

## 6
Generally I try to locate myself near the door that's closest to the exit if I know where I'm getting off if it's a train.  If it's a bus usually closer to the front. If it's not too busy I always try and thank the bus driver when I get off.

## 7

i get on the door closest to me, bus or train (Ottawa does all door boarding on most of its vehicles ). The seat I choose is usually parallel to the body of the vehicle cus i find it easier to get on board. In Canada this is generally the accessibility area. On dubble deckar buses I sit towards the front on the second floor. I usually position myself at the door nearest my destination when getting off, and I put myself around 45° from the door when waiting to enter to let people off first. on some trains with button doors, I press the button before the train arrives a stop to buffer the input and have it open as soon as possible (doesnt work on all trains and doesnt work on any of the buses here or elsewhere ive lived).

I get upset when people crowd the doors or when a bus doesnt have enough standing / circulation room; in Canada we use buses for high capacity routes instead of trains, so a lot of our buses are overcrowded and difficult to circulate. This would be something that would be a bad day for me, or if i have to wait the full headway when transferring between bus and train.  I leave my seat if someone who looks more in need then me appears, or specifically asks, unless I'm in pain. Here its common to have bike racks on  the front of buses, and when using those I ask the bus driver to kneel the bus and I also sit or stand at the front, and press the accessible stop button to indicate I need some extra time when getting off to the driver. Otherwise I try to get off a vehicle while its still moving so the driver can get going faster

when seating is perpendicular and its 2 by 2, and I dont want someone to sit next to me cus I'm in pain or something I will put ma sac in the other seat. Otherwise I sit on the exterior seat so someone else can use the other one. I try to watch someone's body language and expression to identify when they want to leave the seat, because people in Canada dont really like being upfront about their needs
