## VIDEO: HOW ALGORITHMS DIVIDE US
This video is a peek into how machine learning algorithms encode legacies of discrimination, segregation, and eugenics. Further, algorithms reinforce these narratives through the construction of **homophily.**

**Homophily** *n.*
1. *A theory in sociology that people tend to form connections with others who are similar to them in characteristics such as socioeconomic status, values, beliefs, or attitudes.*

2. *The tendency of individuals to associate with others of the same kind. (The American Heritage® Dictionary of the English Language)*

When we engage with new media, our experiences are designed around the idea of homophily, that we will like the things that people similar to us have liked. These algorithms prioritise interests, hobbies, etc. that divide us, so we can be placed into boxes.

**The creation of echo chambers is not an accident, they're a goal.**

This very idea of homophily is a story, with origins in housing projects in the US. This study investigated the impact of social housing on democratic engagement.

I don't really understand everything that happened in the study, she's referring to but: as they grouped people into categories, they discarded all the responses from black residents, and further, ignored the largest category of white respondants --white ambivalents-- in favour of the smaller group, white illiberals.

Essentially, the idea of homophily as being present in our society is a constructed narrative.
